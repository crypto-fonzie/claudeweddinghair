Identity construction · v1

Polished & Pinned

Two things the name gives us for free. “Pinned” means the brass hairpin now illustrates the brand rather than decorating it — the mark and the name explain each other. And the ampersand, set in Instrument Serif's italic, becomes the pivot the whole lockup is built around: the one warm, curved, hand-drawn shape in an otherwise upright and disciplined system.

“by Noelle” is doing quiet strategic work too. A referral business runs on trust in a person, but a personal name alone can't scale past one pair of hands. This structure gets both — an ownable trading name with personal accountability sitting directly beneath it.

01

Primary lockup

Stacked, with the ampersand centred as the pivot. This is the default — deck covers, the site header, signage, packaging, anywhere with room to breathe.

Polished & Pinned Bridal Hair·by Noelle

Primary — stacked

The optical alignment note. “Polished” is eight letters and “Pinned” is six, so identical letterspacing would leave the second line visibly short and the stack lopsided. The two lines carry different tracking — roughly 0.30em and 0.60em — so they finish at the same width and the block reads as a rectangle.

These numbers get you close. Final spacing is corrected by eye at full size, then the whole thing is converted to outlines and saved as the master file.

Over photography the ampersand lifts to a paler brass so it doesn't disappear into a dark image. Everything else goes pure white. Only ever place the lockup over the quiet part of a frame — never across a face or across detailed hair.

02

Horizontal lockup

For anywhere the stack is too tall: the site navigation bar, email signatures, letterhead, the footer of a quote document. The pin leads, the name follows.

03

The pin, alone

Since we dropped the monogram and the bend, the pin carries every small application by itself. That's a heavier load than originally planned, so the proportion is corrected: prongs shortened by about a third, stroke thickened. It now fills a circular crop instead of shrinking to fit one.

Actual size — 44px avatar

Avatar · Instagram, WhatsApp Business, Google Business Profile

Note the stroke weight changes with size. Below about 60px the pin uses the heavier stroke shown here; above it, the finer stroke from the primary lockup. Two drawings, one shape — this is standard practice and it's what keeps a hairline mark from vanishing on a phone.

06

Considered and rejected

Worth recording, because both will get suggested eventually.

  • The pin replacing the “I” in PINNED. Tempting, and wrong. Icon-for-letter substitution reduces legibility at exactly the sizes where the name matters most, it dates badly, and it makes the wordmark unusable in plain text contexts. The pin stays a separate element.
  • A “P&P” monogram. Genuinely good-looking, and the alliteration makes it work. But you dropped the monogram direction, and adding it back gives four competing marks for one small business. If a stamp or embroidery application ever demands something squarer than the pin, revisit it — otherwise leave it.

Misuse

Polished & Pinned Never reset in another typeface — especially a script.
POLISHED & PINNED Never remove the letterspacing. The air is the brand.
POLISHED Never stretch, condense, or distort to fill a space.
POLISHED Never recolour outside the palette. No pink, ever.
POLISHED No shadows, glows, bevels, or outlines.

08

Before this gets locked

Four checks, in order of how expensive they are to discover late.

  • Handle and domain. Check @polishedandpinned on Instagram and the matching .com together, plus your local equivalent. In this category the handle matters more than the domain — take the handle even if the domain needs a variant.
  • Local trading register. Search for an existing hair or beauty business using the name in her region before any money is spent on print.
  • The copy counterbalance. One tension worth naming: “Polished” leans slightly toward done, while the messaging pillar Yours promises she'll still look like herself. That's manageable, but the website and the trial copy should lean hard on Yours so the name reads as “finished properly,” not “made over.”
  • Say it on the phone. Have someone spell it back after hearing it once. “Polished and Pinned” — ampersand or the word “and” in the handle is a real decision, and the spoken version is the one that gets typed into a search bar.
